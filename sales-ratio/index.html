Sales Ratio Analysis

Upload a validated sales file and produce IAAO-grounded ratio statistics, vertical equity diagnostics, and modifier recommendations. Designed for assessor offices to use during the assessment cycle, before certified values are finalized. Not a substitute for the official vertical equity report.

Free, open-source sales ratio analysis tool for Washington county assessors. Distributed under the WSACA umbrella. Single self-contained HTML file — no installation, no backend, no data leaving your browser. Built to IAAO 2025 Standard on Ratio Studies, with editable rules and exportable reports. Created with AI assistance.

Step 1

Upload your sales file

CSV or Excel (.xlsx) file containing your validated sales. Sales should already be screened for arms-length transactions before upload — this tool does not perform sales validation. Multi-parcel sales should be pre-processed before upload (see the "How to read this" panel for the convention); rows with slash-separated values in the neighborhood or DOR-use columns will be dropped automatically.

What data this tool expects

Column headers can use any common naming — auto-detection handles most variations across CAMA systems. You'll confirm the mapping on the next step.

Step 4

Time adjustment

Trend in monthly median assessment ratios (AV ÷ SP) across your study period, fit by weighted least squares with each month weighted by its sales count so sparse months don't dominate the line. Ratios are the IAAO-recommended measure for time trend because assessed values are fixed as of the prior valuation date — drift in median ratio is therefore a direct read on market movement, free from the sales-mix shifts that distort median sale prices. Apply the trend to time-adjust every sale to a common reference date — typically January 1 of the assessment year — or leave it off and analyze unadjusted prices.

Step 5

Overall ratio diagnostics

IAAO standard ratio study statistics for the full sample. These numbers describe the appraisal level (median, mean, weighted mean), uniformity (COD, PRD), and vertical equity (IAAO 2025 Vertical Equity Indicator) of your current assessments relative to validated sales.

Step 6

Vertical equity analysis

Deeper vertical equity diagnostics beyond the headline VEI. The Coefficient of Price-Related Bias (PRB) and Spearman's rank-order correlation test for systematic relationships between assessment ratio and property value. The chart below visualizes each percentile group's median ratio with its 90% confidence interval against the overall sample median — the visualization IAAO recommends to accompany the VEI calculation.

Step 7

Stratified analysis

Same IAAO diagnostics run separately for each level of up to three stratifying variables. Available stratifiers depend on which optional columns you mapped — neighborhood, DOR primary use, land table, year built (decade bins), gross living area (data-driven quartile/decile or fixed 400-sq-ft bins), acreage (fixed bands), quality, and condition. Stratification surfaces uniformity problems that the overall diagnostics smooth over — for example, drift across quality grades or condition codes often indicates a cost-table miscalibration. Strata with fewer than 10 sales are flagged as low-n and treated with caution per IAAO guidance.

Step 8

Modifier recommendations

Per-neighborhood suggested percent adjustments based on editable decision rules. The recommendation tells you how much to move each neighborhood's overall value level — apply it in your own CAMA system however your modifiers work. Rules pre-loaded with sensible defaults; edit any value (or load a saved preset) to see the table re-compute live.

Step 9

Report & export

Package the full analysis for archiving or sharing. The printable report opens in a new tab styled for letter-size paper — use your browser's "Print → Save as PDF" to save it. The Excel workbook contains separate sheets for the summary, modifier recommendations, vertical equity analysis, and each active stratification table.
